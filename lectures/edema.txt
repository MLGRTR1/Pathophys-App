Infusion therapy (IV)
Crystalloids and colloids are plasma volume expanders used to increase a 
depleted circulating volume.
Crystalloid solutions are isotonic plasma volume expanders that contain 
electrolytes.
• sodium chloride 0.9% (normal saline 0.9%) (a side-effect of the excessive use: hyperchloraemic acidosis) 
• in isotonic extracellular dehydration
• in sodium depletion 
• as vehicle or diluent of compatible drugs 
• sodium lactate solutions (Ringer’s lactate solution = Hartmann’s solution) 
• in fluid resuscitation after blood loss
• in metabolic acidosis: metabolic conversion of Ringer’s lactate (no lactic acid in IV) to bicarbonate
• glucose solutions 
• in dehydration
• in malnutrition to prevent ketosis, 
• carrier and diluent for parenteral nutrition (a side-effect of the excessive use: hyponatraemia or polyuria)
• additives such as potassium in hypokalaemia
Colloids are gelatinous solutions that maintain a high osmotic pressure in 
the blood. Colloids (=particles) stay in the intravascular spaces longer than 
crystalloids. side-effects can be hypertension, tachycardia, cardiac failure, 
pulmonary and peripheral oedema or an increased risk of anaphylaxis
• Albumin in cirrhosis and ascites, 
• Dextran antithrombotic (antiplatelet), to reduce blood viscosity, and as a volume expander in hypovolaemia
• Dydroxyethyl starch (or hetastarch):

1. On admission to hospital, profound dehydration and hypotension associated with a 
hyperglycaemic crisis will require stabilisation of blood pressure by rapid fluid 
infusion in preference to slow fluid administration as directed in guidelines. Thus, 
aggressive resuscitation begins with the administration of 1-1.5 L of saline in the 
first hour and should continue until blood pressure is stabilized (Step 1). It is 
imperative that insulin not be started during Step 1, because the addition of insulin 
during rapid fluid administration can cause unpredictable changes in blood glucose 
and serum osmolality. 
2. Once the patient is adequately resuscitated, insulin should be started......
1. Insulin crosses the blood-brain barrier, but insulin is not required for glucose 
transport to most brain cells. 
2. Insulin lowers glycemia because insulin stimulates GLUT4-mediated glucose 
utilization by skeletal cells and adipose tissue. The drop in blood glucose is 
transmitted to the cerebral fluid with a delay (blood-brain barrier) and creates 
an osmotic gradient between the blood and the brain leading to interstitial 
cerebral edema.
1. GLUT1 in glia and brain endothelial cells is independent of insulin
2. GLUT3 in most neurons is independent of insulin
3. GLUT4 in a few selected nuclei but common in adipose tissue, skeletal 
and cardiac cells is sensitive to insulin

• Cerebral ischemia
• Low ATP leading to low function of Na+/K+ pump, cellular retention of Na+, 
Cl- and water accumulation 
• Examples:
• intracerebral hemorrhage 
• ischemic stroke
• acute liver failure: Ammonia accumulates in the blood stream and 
crosses the blood–brain barrier. Hyperammonemia in central 
nervous system (CNS) cells causes oxidative
stress and mitochrondrial dysfunction, leading to damaged function 
of Na+/K+ pump and astrocytic cell swelling. Additionally, ammonia 
is converted to glutamine in CNS cells which acts as 
an osmolyte and draws further water into the cell through osmosis.

Cerebral edema: manifestation
Intracranial Hypertension (ICH)
• defined as ICP greater than 20 mm Hg (normal range: 5–10 mm Hg)
• defined by volume of brain, blood and cerebral fluid
• Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP) - Intracranial Pressure (ICP) = 
Cerebral Perfusion Pressure (CPP)
• poor effect of mean arterial pressure due to autoregulation:
• ↑MAP leads to vasoconstriction in the brain
• ↓MAP leads to vasodilatation in the brain
Stages:
• 100% compensation:  due to ↓production and ↑resorption of cerebrospinal 
fluid (liquor)
• Decreased compensation: ↓ blood volume in the brain, unchanged brain 
perfusion due to ↑blood pressure
• Decompensation: ↑blood pressure but heart rate ↓, ischemia that ↑ CO2 
leading to vasodilatation that even more ↑ ICH, herniation 45

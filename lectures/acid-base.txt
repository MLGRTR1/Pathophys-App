=== PDF PAGE 12 ===
Other sources of H+ production
• Production of free fatty acids from adipose tissue (lipolysis)
• Triglycerides → glycerol + 3 fatty acids + 3H+
In the mitochondria, beta-oxidation generates one acetyl CoA, one 
NADH and one FADH2 for every removal of a two-carbon group from 
fatty acids. Acetyl-CaA enters to the citric acid cycle while NADH and 
FADH2 are used in the electron transport chain where O2 is required.

=== PDF PAGE 13 ===
Production of ketone bodies
• Ketone bodies are produced mainly in the mitochondria of liver cells in 
response to an unavailable of blood glucose, such as during fasting.
• Fatty acids → ketone bodies + nH+
• Ketone bodies are alternative source of energy, e.g. for heart
• The most important ketone bodies are acetone, acetoacetate and beta-
hydroxybutyrate

1 Buffer base and Buffer excess
“Buffer base” (BB) is the sum of bicarbonate and non-bicarbonate 
buffer anions (ie, HCO3
− + K*albumins +K2*hemoglobin).
Base excess is the quantity of base (HCO3
-, in mEq/L) that is above 
or below the normal range of buffer base in the body (22 -28 
mEq/L).

=== PDF PAGE 56 ===
2 Buffer base and Buffer excess
“BE” is defined as the amount of acid or base that must be added in 
vitro to a sample of whole blood to restore the pH to 7.40 while 
PaCO2 is kept constant at 40 mmHg (5.33 kPa). Oxygenated blood 
at PaCO2 40 mmHg (5.33 kPa) and Hb 150 g/L has a BE of zero
Normal Base excess is between -3 and +3mEq/L.
Negative base excess (e.g. BE –8 mmol/L) indicates metabolic 
acidosis 
Abnormally positive base excess (e.g. BE +8 mmol/L) indicates 
metabolic alkalosis.

Delta Ratio
• Delta Ratio is AG scale
• Calculation: (AG – 12)/(24 - [HCO3¯])
• < 0.4 due to NAGMA (normal anion gap metabolic acidosis) 
• 0.4 - 0.8 due to a mixed NAGMA + HAGMA 
• 0.8 - 2.0 due to a pure HAGMA (high anion gap metabolic 
acidosis e.g. Lactic acidosis)
• >2.0 due to a mixed HAGMA + metabolic alkalosis (or pre-
existing compensated respiratory acidosis) (e.g. COPD)

Strong Ion Difference (SID)
• Another approach to assess acid-base disturbance that is based on the 
difference between the concentrations of strong cations and strong anions. 
(Astrup measures pH, HCO3
- and pCO2) 
• SID = [strong cations] – [strong anions]
• apparent SID = SIDa = (Na+ + K+ + Ca2+ + Mg2+) – (Cl– + L-lactate + urate)
• In normal human plasma the SID is 42 mEq/L
• the number of positive and negative ions in a solution must be equal (SID = 0), 
so there are unmeasured anions
• increased SID (>0) leads to alkalosis (increase in unmeasured anions)
• decreased SID (<0) acidosis

Hypocalcaemia (bellow 2.26 mmol/L): causes
• Nutritional deficiencies
• Excessive amounts of dietary phosphorus also bind with calcium, so 
neither mineral is absorbed when such an excess occurs. 
• Hypoparathyroidism (after removal of the parathyroid glands: loss of PTH) 
or Pseudohypoparathyroidism
• Vitamin D deficiency
• Blood transfusions: the citrate solution used in storing whole blood binds 
with calcium and makes it unavailable to the tissues. 
• Renal insufficiency: loss of glomerular filtration with phosphate 
accumulation secondary decreases Ca2+ and secondary 
hyperparathyroidism
• Pancreatitis causes release of lipases into soft tissue spaces, so the free 
fatty acids that are formed bind calcium, causing a decrease in the 
concentration of ionized calcium. 
• Metabolic or respiratory alkalosis: ionized calcium.
• Hypoalbuminemia: total serum calcium or bound calcium in the plasma.

Wilson disease
• An inherited disorder (autosomal recessive pattern) in which excessive 
amounts of copper accumulate in the body: the liver (1st) than brain and 
eyes.
– diminished biliary excretion of copper
– failure to insert copper into ceruloplasmin (a glycoprotein that transports copper in the 
blood)
• Excesses of copper generate free radicals that disrupt cellular organelles
– clumsiness, tremors, difficulty walking, speech problems, impaired 
thinking ability, depression, anxiety, and mood swings. 
– mild liver disease: yellowing of the skin or of the eyes (jaundice), 
cirrhosis
– green-to-brownish ring, called the Kayser-Fleischer ring
dark rings that appear to 
encircle the iris of the eye

Two forms of beriberi
• Wet beriberi affects the cardiovascular system resulting in 
cardiomyopathy (dilated cardiomegaly, biventricular heart failure): 
hyperdynamic circulation (systemic vasodilation that is compensated 
by high cardiac output and fast heart rate, shortness of breath), 
and leg swelling.
• Dry beriberi affects the nervous system by demyelination resulting 
in peripheral neuropathy and than muscle wasting
– Tingling or numbness of the hands and feet and partial paralysis
– Symmetrical and neuropathy: difficulty rising from squatting position
– Confusion and pain
– Impaired sensory perception and motor weakness, poor reflexes

=== PDF PAGE 11 ===
Dry beriberi: Wernicke encephalopathy 
Wernicke encephalopathy (CNS) is characterized by the triad
• ophthalmoplegia: refers to weakness (-paresis) or paralysis (-plegia) 
of one or more extraocular muscles which are responsible for eye 
movements and later expanded to other eye movement disorders 
(nystagmus)
• ataxia: lack of voluntary coordination of muscle movements
• confusion

Lipoprotein metabolism disorders


=== PDF PAGE 36 ===
• A lipoprotein is an assembly whose primary purpose is to 
transport hydrophobic lipid molecules in water (blood).
• A lipoprotein consists of 
• lipid center (triglycerides and cholesteryl esters = a dietary lipid, is 
an ester of cholesterol)
• outer shell, with the hydrophilic portions oriented outward toward the 
plasma and lipophilic portions oriented inward toward the lipid 
center. (phospholipids, cholesterol and embedded apolipoproteins that 
determines its fate).
Lipoprotein

=== PDF PAGE 37 ===
• Chylomicrons transport exogenous (dietary) products from the intestines to 
other locations in the body. 
• Nascent chylomicron: The enterocytes use fatty acids 
and monoacylglycerides from the chime and transform again into 
triglycerides and add apolipoprotein B-48
• Mature chylomicrons: Nascent chylomicrons interact with HDL particles, 
resulting in HDL donation of apolipoprotein C-II and apolipoprotein E to the 
nascent chylomicron. 
• Via apolipoprotein C-II, mature chylomicrons activate lipoprotein 
lipase (LPL), an enzyme on endothelial cells lining the blood vessels in 
the adipose (mainly) and muscle (less) tissues. LPL catalyzes 
the hydrolysis of triglycerides that releases glycerol and fatty acids. 
• They are absorbed in peripheral tissues (adipose and muscle).
• Chylomicron remnants: When a large portion of the triglyceride core has 
been hydrolyzed, chylomicron remnants are formed and are taken up by 
the liver (apolipoprotein E).
Exogenous pathway of Lipoproteins

=== PDF PAGE 38 ===
• Very-low-density lipoprotein (VLDL): is a type of lipoprotein made by 
the liver (e.g. after the excessive incomes of sugar or ethanol). VLDL 
transports endogenous triglycerides and circulates in blood. Lipoprotein 
lipase (LPL) removes the triglycerides  from VLDL for storage or energy 
production. 
• Intermediate-density lipoprotein (IDL) or VLDL remnants: As more and more 
triglycerides are removed from the VLDL, it becomes IDL. Via an interaction 
between apolipoprotein E and the remnant receptor, IDL are absorbed by 
the liver or escape to produce LDL. 
• IDL remnants called low-density lipoproteins (LDL): If their cholesterol 
content becomes greater than the content of triglyceride, IDL becomes LDL. 
The majority of LDL content (cholesterol) is either stored, secreted in bile 
(free cholesterol) used for cell membrane structure (cholesterol in the 
cellular memrane), or converted into other products such as steroid 
hormones or bile acids. Macrophages remover small part of the circulating 
LDL. 
Endogenous pathway of Lipoproteins

=== PDF PAGE 39 ===
• HDL is produced by the liver. It is the densest because it contains the 
highest proportion of protein to lipids.
• HDL remove  the tissue cholesterol from e.g. dead cells and transfer 
it to VLDL and then LDL .
• HDL is able to prevent the process of oxidation of LDL and the 
subsequent inflammatory processes seen after oxidation.
• HDL has the ability to bind LPS, creating HDL-LPS complexes to 
neutralize the harmful effects in the body and clear the LPS from the 
body.
• LDL contains apolipoprotein B (apoB), which allows LDL to bind to 
different tissues, such as the artery wall 
• if they have been damaged by high blood sugar levels.
• if oxidised, the LDL can become trapped in the proteoglycans, 
preventing its removal by HDL cholesterol efflux. Normal functioning.

Defects in Amino Acid Metabolism
• An enzymatic deficiency are associated with increased blood

and with the appearance of the amino acid in the urine.
• Examples:
• Phenylketonuria (PKU)
• Homocystinuria (HCU)

=== PDF PAGE 41 ===
• Genetic defect in enzymes that convert homocysteine to methionine or other 
of the amino acids (an inherited autosomal recessive trait) 
• Pathophysiology: an accumulation of the amino acid homocysteine in 
the serum
• Signs and symptoms:
• Intellectual disability
• Musculoskeletal diseases: osteoporosis, abnormally-shaped chest and 
bone and joint problems
• Vascular diseases: Homocysteine binds to the endothelium of the 
blood vessels and activate signaling pathways leading to the release 
of pro-inflammatory molecules. This may cause the following :
• Extensive atheroma formation at a young age which affects many 
arteries but not the coronary arteries
• Intravascular thrombosis
• Symptoms of homocystinuria can also be caused by a deficiency of vitamins 
B6, B12, or folate
Homocystinuria (HCU)

=== PDF PAGE 42 ===
Phenylketonuria (PKU):
• Mutations of the phenylalanine hydroxylase (PAH) gene results in 
phenylalanine hydroxylase deficiency and the inability of the body to 
convert the essential amino acid phenylalanine to tyrosine, leading to the 
accumulation of phenylalanine in the serum.
• The accumulation of phenylalanine: brain growth slow, intellectual 
disability, mental retardation, jerking movements, seizures, skin 
rashes and attention problems.
• The lack of tyrosine : blond hair, blue eyes, and fair skin (lack of 
melanin)

=== PDF PAGE 44 ===
• Hyperglycemia:  7mmol/L 
• Manifestation:
• Glycosurie if serum glucose 10mmol/L, dehydration
• Acute: Hyperglycemic and hyperosmolar coma in Type II diabetes mellitus
• Chronic: AGE advanced glycation endproducts damage vessel wall, hemoglobin 
glycation, polyol pathway increasing sorbitol in some organs, ROS production
• The 1st step in the sorbitol pathway is the conversion of glucose to sorbitol. 
The 2nd step in this pathway is the conversion of sorbitol into fructose via 
the enzyme sorbitol dehydrogenase. A point of concern is that not all tissues 
have the enzyme sorbitol dehydrogenase, and these include the retina, 
kidneys, and Schwann cells. As a result, diabetic patients with ↑ glucose 
levels accumulate sorbitol to toxic levels within these organs.
• Causes of hyperglycemia:
• Diabetes mellitus Type I
• Diabetes mellitus Type II
• Insulin resistance that accompany many diseases, e.g. infection
• Increased corticoids: Cushing‘s syndrome or drugs
• Adenoma of pituitary gland: acromegaly (STH)
• Glucagonoma: a very rare tumor of the pancreatic alpha cells that results in the 
overproduction of the hormone, glucagon.

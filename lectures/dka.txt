Diabetic ketoacidosis
• Serious complication of diabetes mellitus due to an absolute or 
relative deficiency of insulin and an increase in the levels of insulin 
counterregulatory hormones (glucagon)
• This is most common in individuals with type 1 diabetes but can 
occur in those with type 2 diabetes as well. Younger age is also a 
risk factor.
• The most common precipitating factor for DKA is intercurrent illness, 
such as infection, trauma, surgery, or myocardial infarction.
• Excess production of keto acids: (acetoacetic acid, hydroxybutyric
acid and acetone) 

=== PDF PAGE 3 ===
Ketone bodies, an alternative source of 
energy 
• Physiology: 
– They are produced from acetyl coenzyme A mainly in the 
mitochondria within hepatocytes and used peripherally as an 
energy source when glucose is not readily available. (e.g. The 
brain cannot utilize fatty acids for energy when blood glucose levels 
become compromised.)
– They are always present in the blood and their levels increase 
during fasting and prolonged exercise. 
• Diabetic ketoacidosis:
– A high concentration and it is  a cause of metabolic acidosis with 
high AG.
– Fatty acids are degraded by β-oxidation faster than the formed 
acetyl coenzyme A can enter the Krebs cycle. Excess acetyl 
coenzyme A gives ketone bodies

=== PDF PAGE 4 ===
Insulin and glucagon
• Physiology: 
– Insulin 
• stimulates glycogenesis and glycolysis 
• inhibits lipolysis, gluconeogenesis and glycogenolysis, 
– Glucagon
• increases blood glucose level by stimulating glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis in the liver
• Diabetic ketoacidosis:
– insulin deficiency:
• lipolysis is enhanced and there is an increase in the amount of fatty acids 
delivered to the liver. 
– The consequence is increased gluconeogenesis (generation 
of glucose from certain non-carbohydrate carbon substrates) 
contributing to hyperglycemia. 
– increases production of ketone bodies (beta-oxidation generates one 
acetyl CoA for every removal of a two-carbon group from the fatty acid 
chain. ) by the mitochondria of the liver at a rate that exceeds peripheral 
use. 
– Increased glucagon 
• contribute to activation of gluconeogenesis

=== PDF PAGE 5 ===
Clinical manifestation
• Polyuria and dehydration result from the osmotic diuresis due to 
hyperglycemia and ↑glucose in urine.
• Although the serum K+ concentration may appear normal or elevated 
because of volume contraction (there is not a shift of K + out of the cell into 
the blood caused by metabolic acidosis with ketone bodies), there is the 
total body deficiency of K+ due to:
– polyuria 
– ↑ urinary anion of hydroxybutyric acid: It is not reabsorbed and its neg. charge attracts K+
to urine) 
– shift K+ to renal tubular cells support K+ excretion.
• Others: Kussmaul respirations with acetone odor on the breath, postural 
dizziness, central nervous system depression, ketonuria, anorexia, 
nausea, abdominal pain and thirst. 
• Treatment: intravenous administration of
– insulin to decrease glucose levels
– fluids to replace lost fluid volume. 
– K+, Na+and phosphorus
• After the administration of insulin, the concentration of β-hydroxybutyrate 
promptly begins to decrease, and after a slight increase, acetoacetate 
concentration also begins to decrease.

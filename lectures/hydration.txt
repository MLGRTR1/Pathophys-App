Effective volume
• When the effective circulating blood volume is compromised, the 
condition is often referred to as hypovolemia.
• Effective volume — it is monitored by sensors in the renal afferent 
glomerular arterioles (for renin), carotid sinus (for antidiuretic 
hormone, ADH), and atria and ventricles (for natriuretic peptides) that 
respond to changes in pressure (baroreceptors), not volume.
• By contrast to the baroreceptors, osmoreceptor in hypothalamus are 
important for thirst sensation and to induce secretion of antidiuretic

Primary aldosteronism (primary hyperaldosteronism)
• It is increased aldosterone originally
• Causes: 
• Benign aldosterone-producing adrenal adenoma (Conn's syndrome)
• Bilateral adrenal nodular hyperplasia: Because cortisol production is low, ACTH concentration increases and causes 
adrenal hyperplasia.
• Adrenal carcinomas (rare)
Secondary hyperaldosteronism
• It is increased renin originally and secondarily  increased aldosterone and 
renin
• Causes: cirrhosis, ectopic production of renin, coarctation of aorta (narrow 
aorta), stenosis of renal arteria, decreased cardiac output in cardiac failure 
(decreased circulating blood volume). During pregnancy, estrogen  
stimulates the production of renin and angiotensin. In hyperthyroidism, 
thyroid hormones increase renin.
Pseudohyperaldosteronism 
• low aldosterone
• activation of the aldosterone receptor without an increase in aldosterone.
• Cushing’s syndrome (high cortisol stimulated receptor for 
aldosterone)
• High dose steroid therapy
• Liddle syndrome, an autosomal dominant genetic disorder
Hyperaldosteronism
